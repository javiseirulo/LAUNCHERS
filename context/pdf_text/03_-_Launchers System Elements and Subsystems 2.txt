                    LV Propulsion: Intro
▪ In the combustion chamber the
  flow is almost at rest. Turbopump
  before the chamber generates a
  high temperature fluid and
  increases the feeding pressure.

▪ In the first part of the nozzle
  (convergent), the flow is subsonic.
  The temperature decreases and
  flux velocity is accelerated.

▪ In the throat area the flow is sonic
  (Mach = 1)

▪ At this time, the flow can be
  accelerated by means of a
  diverging duct. In this expansion
  the flow reaches supersonic
  condition.

              LV Propulsion: Solid Propulsion
▪ Basic Principle:
     ▪   Fuel and oxidizer are mixed in a solid propellant grain,
         which is manufactured and stored inside the motor
         casing.
     ▪   Igniter generates initial E for oxidizer + fuel contained
         in the grain to react ->surface regression starts (based
         on grain geometry)


▪ Advantages
     ▪   High density: easily and effectively storable
     ▪   Chemical stability (long term storage)
     ▪   Simplicity: low cost, high production rates, reduced
         maintenance
     ▪   Total impulse is kept constant (important for staging
         sizing, but lack of flexibility wrt liquid propulsion)




▪ Drawbacks:
     ▪   Low Specific Impulse
     ▪   Very difficult to terminate the thrust (once started is
         difficult to stop) (gunpowder)
     ▪   Difficult to control final velocity reached
     ▪   While in operation, explosion and fire potential is
         larger, failure can be catastrophic
     ▪   Complicated TVC system (Thrust Vector Control):
         difficult gimbaling, noozles for solid prop are big and
         actuated by vanes and flexible nozzle (limited rotation)

       LV Propulsion: Solid Propulsion
▪ Five phases can be distinguished:


    ▪ Phase 1: reach rapidly a high thrust level and limit the
      gravity loss (ascent as fast as possible to be able to
      rotate to reduce gravity losses)

    ▪ Phase 2: Thrust level is reduced to avoid higher peaks
      in dynamic pressure (max Q)

    ▪ Phase 3: Thrust is increased just passing max Q. The
      third phase ends when the maximum tolerated
      acceleration is reached.

    ▪ Phase 4: During the phase four, the thrust level has to
      decrease in order to follow the reduction of the
      launcher mass and avoid that the acceleration exceed
      the levels previously reached.

    ▪ Phase 5: Corresponds to the sharp drop-off of the
      pressure in the combustion chamber and the
      corresponding reduction in burn rate.

                        LV Liquid Propulsion
4 main architectures:


     ▪ The pressure-fed cycle (low thrust):
       A separate gas supply, usually helium,
       pressurizes the propellant tanks to force fuel
       and oxidizer to the combustion chamber




     ▪ Expander cycle (moderate thrust<300kN):
       Fuel is used to cool the engine's combustion
       chamber, picking up heat and changing phase.
       The heated gaseous fuel then powers the
       engine's pumps and turbine before being
       injected into the combustion chamber and
       burned

                             LV Liquid Propulsion
▪ Gas-generator cycle (Open Combustion).
  The gas generator cycle is a power cycle of a bipropellant rocket engine.
  Some of the propellant is burned in a gas-generator and the resulting hot
  gas is used to power the engine's pumps. The gas is then exhausted. Lower
  Efficiency but lower complexity

▪ Staged combustion cycle (Closed cycle).
  Some of the propellant is burned in a pre-burner and the resulting hot gas is
  used to power the engine's turbines and pumps.
  The exhausted gas is then injected into the main combustion chamber,
  along with the rest of the propellant, and combustion is completed.
  High Thrust and high efficiency but higher complexity

  Launch Vehicle- Liquid Propulsion
▪ Advantages
    ▪ Usually higher specific impulse 𝐼𝑠𝑝
    ▪ Throttling and restarting capabilities
    ▪ Cutoff impulse can be controllable with thrust
      termination device (better control of vehicle
      terminal velocity)
    ▪ Can be reusable
    ▪ Can modify operating conditions during firing to
      prevent some failures
    ▪ Simplified TVC (less mass than solid prop so
      higher control authority): 2 axis gimballing
      system.
▪ Drawbacks:
    ▪ Complex design, more parts or components,
      more things to go wrong.
    ▪ Cryogenic propellants cannot be stored for long
      periods
    ▪ More difficult to control combustion instability
    ▪ Requires more volume due to lower average
      propellant density
    ▪ Sloshing in tank can cause a flight stability
      problem

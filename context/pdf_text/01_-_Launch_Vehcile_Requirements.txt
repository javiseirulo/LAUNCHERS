  Requirements in System Engineering
▪ Requirements are the foundation of any
  engineering effort


▪ A requirement is a statement that identifies a
  product or process characteristic, which is
  unambiguous verifiable, measurable and
  necessary for product or process acceptability


▪ The cornerstone of systems approach is the
  correct translation of need into requirements


▪ The vast majority of project that end up
  derailing in terms of poor performances,
  schedule delays and costs overruns are
  inundated with inappropriate or ill-defined
  requirements

  A significant Example: FASTRAC Engine and X-34
▪ The FASTRAC Engine Project

   ▪ In the middle of the 1990s NASA was
     pushing to lower the cost and
     development time of access to space

   ▪ Propulsion costs and development
     have a relevant impact on overall
     budget and schedule

   ▪ Propulsion community within NASA
     MSFC (Marshall Space Flight Center)
     sought to go from a ‘clean sheet’
     engine design to having the engine in
     a test stand in 2 years: COTS;
     simplified design, lower manufacturing
     costs. It was a standalone product
     development prgram

       A significant Example: FASTRAC Engine @NASA
▪ The X-34 VEHICLE

   ▪ At approximately the same time as the
     FASTRAC engine development, NASA released
     a NASA Research Announcement (NRA) for a
     reusable flight x-vehicle to demonstrate
     technologies, the X-34.

   ▪ The X-34 vehicle was intended only as a flight
     demonstration vehicle to test some of the key
     features of reusable launch vehicle operation
     (released by the aircraft)

   ▪ NASA selected Orbital Sciences Corporation in
     1996, awarding it a fixed price, $49.5 million
     contract.

   ▪ Under the contract, Orbital was given lead
     responsibility for vehicle design, fabrication,
     integration, and initial flight testing for powered
     flight of the X-34 test vehicle

                             Conclusion
▪ From a vehicle perspective, it was reasonable to expect that the FASTRAC
  engine would be modified for the X-34 application.


▪ The MSFC engine team believed that they had been directed to offer the engine
  to the X-34 “as is”, which was understood as no vehicle requirements would be
  flowed down to impact engine development


▪ As an example, the X-34 was to be launched in a horizontal configuration, a
  requirement not initially considered by the engine development team, which
  tested vertically.


▪ The requirements from the X-34 were not flown down to FASTRAC because
  the engine was originally conceived as a standalone low-cost demonstrator


▪ Ultimately, concerns arose over the impact of engine orientation on such items
  as firing the gas generator, engine chill down and propellants and pressuring
  bleed systems.


▪ Powered flight never achieved

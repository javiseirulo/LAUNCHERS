                             LV Bending model
 The nozzles of gimbaling engines further
  deflect from their trim positions in order to
  guide and stabilize the vehicle against
  disturbances.
 These additional deflections generate small
  components of thrust force variations
  perpendicular to the velocity vector that affect
  the vehicle attitude and direction
 The intended deflections, however, are
  corrupted by structural flexibility at the engine
  gimbal
 Total Nozzle deflection consists of two
  components: a rotation at the gimbal, plus
  structure deformation
 We are interested in knowing separately the
  gimbal rotation (rigid one), so we shall
  model the flexible body rotation contribution
  to filter it (thanks to its modelling we can
  understand the frequency at which it
  happens and then design filters around
  that frequency for the Navigation solution
  provision)
 Important: the only sensors are at the Avionic
  Bay, there is no sensor to really measure at
  the TVC

      In the real world, signal given by the sensors are filtered (Low Pass Filter or Band Rejection Filters at

      In the simulation world, the measurement given by IMU is obtained by adding the rigid and the flexible
       one:        is modeled on top of        . The objective is to try to be as much as possible as close to
       reality (what real sensors would measure) and assess our control authority.
 More specifically, the measures taken against bending mode effects in real world are:
      Place the IMU (and in general the Avionic Bay) at bending nodes points: reduce accelerations values
      Filter out the bending modes frequencies in Navigation to obtain the true rigid acceleration
      Filter at Contol sent to TVC, to avoid exciting bending frequency modes (at Guidance, rigid
       dynamics are assumed, since the frequency at which it works is lower

 The forces acting on a vehicle from a sloshing
  propellant in a partially filled tank (accelerations
  coming from the vehicle         and      The liquid
  movement is ecxited and it reacts by exerting
  some reaction forces to the LV (forces we
  want to model)


 The model is a pendulum that is suspended from
  a pivot point in the tank centerline, a little below
  the liquid surface.


 The pendulum mass represents the sloshing part
  of the propellant near the surface. This mass
  moves following a oscillator, and therefore exerts
  a reaction force on the LV


 The remaining of the propellant that is not
  sloshing is assumed to be rigid and its mass is
  located at a fixed point further down in the
  centerline of the tank
